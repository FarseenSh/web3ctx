## 2 · 🔴 G4, diagnosed rather than recorded

`_beforeTokenTransfer` lives in **four** sections of the OZ recipe — the header (2,991 B),
`interface` (2,068), `version_discriminator` (2,095), `working_example` (3,432) and
`common_mistakes` (11,768). **Selection took `deployments` · `approval_flow` · `scope_notes`.**

⚠ **`_update` survived, so the correct column held** — what disappeared is the *labelled
coexistence* the OZ anchor exists to show. The payload stopped saying that v5 deleted the old hook.

⭐ **The honest reading:** on the anchor whose recipe is richest and whose question is most

ordinary English (`custom`, `logic`, `every`, `token`, `transfer`) and they do not point at the
code-bearing sections. **The scorer is good where the question names an identifier and weak where
it does not**, which is the same gap F68's floor is about, one layer in.

## 4 · G8 — the count, and what it does and does not mean

**Served-answer presence: prefix-truncate 15/24 → section-select 12/24.**

The marker per recipe is **derived, never hand-picked** — the most frequent camelCase/dotted
identifier outside the metadata header — because choosing the input data is the one thing a
measurement must never let the measurer do.

⚠ **The metric is query-INDEPENDENT and the mechanism is query-DEPENDENT**, and that gap is most of
the drop. The probe asks each recipe a mechanical question (*"how do I wallet connector with
wagmi"*), which does not name `metaMask` — so selection has no reason to choose its section, while
a prefix that happens to start there keeps it. The pre-registration said this in advance: *"some
recipes' answer is not a token the question repeats, and the count exists to show how far selection
carries — not to be a score."*

🔴 **It is still a drop on a reasonable proxy and it is not waved away.** Read beside G1/G2: on the
three questions where the query names the answer, selection wins; averaged over questions that do
not, it loses.

## 5 · What was built, and what it cost to find out

- **BM25 with IDF over top-level YAML sections** — term saturation (`k1=1.2`), length normalisation
  against the section average (`b=0.5`), and IDF over the recipe's own sections.
- **Exact 0/1 knapsack** for the fill.

🔴 **Both greedy orderings were measured failing, in opposite directions**, and neither could be
chosen without tuning to the test:

| ordering | viem | erc-7702 |
|---|---|---|
| by **score** | ✔ | ✘ — the best section (5,715 B) does not fit, and the room went elsewhere, missing `version_discriminator` **by 0.08 of a score point** |
| by **density** | ✘ — small sections crowded out `interface` | ✔ |
| **exact knapsack** | ✔ | ✔ |

⭐ Picking between two heuristics by which anchor they happen to pass is not a decision, it is a
coincidence. With ~20 sections and a ~6,500-character budget the exact DP is ~130,000 integer
operations, so the question could simply be removed.

⚠ **Two scorer defects were found by measuring, not by reading:** `hits / log(len)` ranked a 744-byte
`required_imports` above the three sections carrying `0xef0100`; and omitting IDF let `contract`,
`delegate` and `eoa` — in nearly every section — outvote `extcodesize`, which is in one.

## 6 · Three test defects this cycle, each caught by falsifying

| | |
|---|---|
| the elision test compared the body against **the very constant under test** — emptying `ELISION` left `''.includes('')`, true for every string | **a test that imports its own expected value cannot fail** |
| the knapsack test used a hand-built fixture where **both greedy orderings agreed**, so substituting greedy left it green | replaced with **optimality against brute force, swept over 33 budgets** |
| the length-normalisation test passed the section's **own** length as the average, making the normaliser 1 on both sides | a scorer compared against itself cannot show a length effect |

⚠ **And the anchor overwrote its own baseline.** The F72 run cached its payload over the frozen OZ
one, so the neutrality test failed against a baseline that had moved. Restored from git — the same
hazard the anchor's own header warns about, arriving from the other side.

## 7 · What would make it shippable, stated but NOT attempted

Not proposed, not scheduled — the gate failed and that is the report:

1. **Never drop a section the previous behaviour served** — a monotonic floor, so no anchor can go
   backwards. It changes the byte budget's meaning and needs a ruling.
2. **Expand the query with the recipe's own declared identifiers** before scoring, so a prose
   question reaches code sections. That is ingest-side expansion on the hot path — golden rule 6
   territory.
3. **Curator convention: recipes lead with the code.** Costs the server nothing and is the
   curator's to rule, exactly as F72 first recorded.

# 4 · THE COMMENT-STRIPPING RENDERER — MEASURED, FAILS ITS GATE, SKIPPED. 2026-08-20.

Item 3 of the final pre-launch cycle, offered **optional and gated**:

> *"a serve-time renderer dropping comment lines from recipe bodies — takes the remaining 324
> inline-reference tokens with zero risk to sources. Gate on the same clauses that killed
> section-select: no twin loses its label, no protocol caveat leaves the payload (sample-verified),
> OZ anchor unchanged. If it fails its gate, skip it and record post-launch — do not iterate on it
> today."*

**It fails the second clause, and it fails it structurally.** Reproduce with
`node --experimental-strip-types tools/probe-comment-strip.ts`.

## 4.1 · What it would remove

| | measured |
|---|---|
| served body chars, 24 recipes at budget 4,000 | **142,610** |
| of which **comment lines** | **48,369 — 33.9%, ≈13,820 tokens** |
| served lines · comment lines | 2,782 · **535** |
| 🔴 **served comment lines carrying a protocol caveat** | **≥ 67** |

⚠ **The estimate in the offer was 324 tokens; the measured figure is ≈13,820 — 40× larger.** Stated
plainly because it cuts *for* the proposal on the saving side and is the reason the harm side had to
be counted at all. **A change worth 324 tokens would not have been worth a gate.**

## 4.2 · 🔴 Why it fails: the saving and the harm are the SAME BYTES

The lines a stripper removes are the lines the curator wrote to stop a reader assuming something.
Verbatim, from the served prefixes:

- `# ⚠ THIS RECIPE DOES NOT SAY WHICH VERSION THE SECOND ONE IS. Nothing measured established…`
- `# ⚠ ETHEREUM ONLY. The README's table gives the same address on many chains and only mainnet…`
- `# ⚠ TWO ADDRESSES, AND THEY ARE NOT ALTERNATIVES. The first is what the README gives and what…`

⭐ **The first of those is a version-blindness caveat inside a payload whose entire purpose is
version-truth.** A renderer that removed it would ship the address without the sentence saying which
chain it is on — **producing, in our own payload, the market failure the product exists to fix.**
That is not a tuning risk; it is the product inverted.

**67 is a FLOOR, not a total** — it is what a deliberately conservative pattern matches, and a wider
one returns more. **No pattern returns zero**, which is precisely why the decision does not depend
on the pattern.

## 4.3 · Ruled

🔴 **SKIPPED. Not iterated on, per the instruction.** Recorded **post-launch**, and it inherits
R-AB's standing: **no new serving-side attempt on the recipe-body residual without an owner ruling.**

⚠ **It is not merely "another failed packaging candidate."** C1, C2 and batched lookup failed on
*cost-benefit*. This one fails on **safety of the payload**, and the two are different verdicts: a
cheaper variant of a cost-benefit failure is worth proposing later, a cheaper variant of *this*
removes the same sentences. **If comment tokens are ever to be recovered, it is by the curator
deciding which comments are notes and which are caveats — the R-AB shape — never by a renderer that
cannot tell them apart.**

⭐ **Nothing was built.** The probe counts; there is no stripper in the tree. This is the first of
the four candidates to be **refused before implementation**, on a count taken first.
